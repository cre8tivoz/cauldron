# Code of conduct

Cauldron is a small, friendly project. Everyone's welcome to turn up, ask questions and have a go. A few simple rules keep it that way.

## What we expect

- **Be decent.** Talk to people the way you'd want to be talked to.
- **Assume good faith.** Most people are trying to help. Give them the benefit of the doubt.
- **Critique the work, not the person.** "This breaks at 375px" is useful. "This is stupid" isn't.
- **No harassment.** No insults, threats, hateful language or unwanted personal attention — not in issues, not in pull requests, not anywhere in the project.

## What isn't welcome

- Harassment or bullying of any kind.
- Personal attacks, insults and put-downs.
- Discriminatory language or jokes, about race, gender, sexuality, disability, religion or anything else.
- Posting someone's private details, or doxxing.
- Derailing threads, spamming, or deliberately winding people up.
- Passing someone else's work off as your own.

If a discussion's getting heated, step back. Maintainers may step in early to cool things down.

## What maintainers may do

To keep the project usable, a maintainer may:

- **Edit** a comment or issue to strip out abusive or irrelevant material.
- **Lock** a thread that's going in circles or attracting abuse.
- **Remove** comments, issues or pull requests that break these rules.
- **Ban** someone from the repository, for a while or for good.

We'll keep it proportionate. You won't always get a warning first, and there's no debate about it in the thread.

## Raising a concern privately

If something's wrong and you don't want to say it in public:

- Use GitHub's **reporting tools** on the offending comment or content (the `...` menu, then **Report**).
- Or contact the maintainer through GitHub at **[@cre8tivoz](https://github.com/cre8tivoz)**.

Please don't open a public issue to report a person. Tell us what happened and where — that's enough for us to deal with it fairly.
